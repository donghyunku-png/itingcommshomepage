AI search rankings: do not judge performance from one measurement

A practical guide to repeated GEO observation, comparable prompts, metric denominators and careful before-and-after interpretation

ITING Communications2026-11-18Updated 2026-11-18

Your brand appeared in an AI answer yesterday but disappeared today. A report says your ranking improved after a website edit. Are those observations enough to declare success or failure? First check whether the measurement was repeated under comparable conditions and how its denominator was defined.

Research calls for better measurement, not abandoning it

An April 2026 study by Schulte, Bleeker and Kaufmann describes variation in sources and brands across runs, prompts and time, supporting repeated observation. Its four industries and four engines in a Swiss-German setting do not establish results for every Korean industry. Original study

Sielinski’s preprint, released in July and revised in August, proposes checking both ranking stability and whether differences can be resolved relative to measurement uncertainty. It studies 30 platform-topic combinations rather than prescribing one collection count for every context. The author is affiliated with measurement vendor IQRush. This proposal is not a universally validated standard or proof that a product is superior. Latest public version · Author affiliation and explanation

These are not announcements released today. We revisited the public sources on November 18, 2026 to consider practical reporting implications.

Put conditions next to the number

The following is ITING Communications’ practical recommendation informed by the research, not a single standard validated by the papers or a list of current GEO Inside features.

  • Prompt set: Use the same questions before and after a change. Label a changed question set as a separate comparison group.
  • Environment: Record the engine and access method, language and region settings, execution times and observation window. Do not silently combine different environments into one ranking.
  • Repetition and failures: Separate requested runs, successful responses and citation-bearing responses. Do not count an error as a brand absence.
  • Metric definition: Distinguish brand mentions from website citations. State the numerator, denominator and deduplication rule.
  • Interpretation: Separate observed values, variation across runs and unresolved differences. Do not invent confidence intervals that were never calculated.

For example, eight brand mentions in 20 successful responses and four website citations in ten citation-bearing responses both equal 40%, but they are different metrics. These are hypothetical numbers, not client results.

Read before-and-after comparisons carefully

Log the edited pages and implementation date, then repeat observations using comparable prompts and settings on both sides of the change. Check whether the direction persists rather than selecting one favorable run. Disclose any changes in engine or question mix during the comparison period.

Repetition helps interpretation, but does not automatically establish that an edit caused the change. Without a control group or examination of other factors, distinguish “the observation changed after the edit” from “the edit caused it.” When differences remain unresolved, report that more observation is needed.

Keep visibility and business outcomes separate

AI mentions and citations are not visits or inquiries. Review a clearly defined visibility report alongside actual website and inquiry data without claiming an unverified causal connection. See our guide to estimated versus observed AI search clicks.
