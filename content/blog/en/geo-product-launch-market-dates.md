# A Debut Is Not a Sale: How to Check Product Launch Dates in AI Answers

Two official announcements give two dates for the same product. Does one have to be wrong? Not if they describe different events in different markets. When an AI answer says a product “launched,” first check **where, when and what actually began**.

## On sale in Korea, unveiled in North America

Genesis announced that **the GV80 Hybrid went on sale in Korea on October 6, 2026**. Its account of a **November 18 event in Los Angeles, local date** describes the vehicle’s North American debut. The manufacturer said that North American pricing, specifications and availability would be announced closer to launch. That release does not establish a North American on-sale date. The **G80 Hybrid sedan**, which made its global debut at the same event, is a different model from the GV80 SUV. [1][2]

So it is consistent for the vehicle to go on sale in Korea in October and make its first North American appearance in November. Check the relevant market’s retail announcement before treating a debut as availability.

## Make the milestone explicit on your own site

Open each product announcement with the **exact model, market, milestone and date**. Do not collapse the start of reservations, the unveiling, the first day of sales and the start of deliveries into one date. If a date is pending, say so and link to the official local source. Keep product milestones separate from the page’s publication and update dates. Google also advises against using the date of the event itself as the article’s publication or modification date. That is guidance about date display in Google Search, not a recipe for AI citations. [3]

## Two questions to try in a GEO review

1. “As of November 20, 2026, **when did the GV80 Hybrid go on sale in Korea?**”
2. “As of that same date, **does the official North American debut release give a local on-sale date?**”

A good answer gives the Korean on-sale date in the first case and distinguishes the debut from a sale date **not stated in the source** in the second. Record the engine, exact prompt, observation date and original citation. Mark an answer as a separate issue when it has a citation but mismatches the market, model or sales milestone.
