60 MW or 110 MW? How to Check the Scope of a Number in an AI Answer

A practical GEO check for matching each figure to the right entity, scope, and status, illustrated by reporting on an AI data center campus.

ITING Communications2026-11-27Updated 2026-11-27

An AI answer can link to a source that contains the right number and still get the claim wrong. The number may describe a single new facility, while the answer treats it as the total for several facilities, or vice versa.

60 MW for Ansan 2, 110 MW for the campus plan

According to reporting on November 26, KT Cloud's proposed Ansan 2 AI data center has a stated incoming electrical capacity of 60 MW. The planned campus totals 110 MW once the new site is combined with the existing Ansan AI data center. The reporting gives 2030 as a service launch target for Ansan 2. Neither “Ansan 2 alone is 110 MW” nor “110 MW is already operational” is supported by those reports. [1] [2]

Incoming electrical capacity does not tell us the actual power allocated to servers or how many GPUs are installed. Those figures need separate evidence.

Check three things alongside the number

  1. Entity: Which product, facility, or market does the number describe? Check for similarly named entities.
  2. Scope and unit: Is this one facility or several? Even values in the same unit can refer to a facility's electrical capacity or to server power.
  3. Date and status: Is the figure a plan, a confirmed milestone, or a currently operating value? A target date is not an actual opening date.

A useful review prompt is: “Give me the incoming electrical capacity of Ansan 2, the planned capacity of the combined Ansan campus, and the target service launch for Ansan 2. Cite the evidence for each separately.” This is an illustrative prompt, not a result measured on any AI engine.

Put the conditions next to the figure on your website

For specifications or capacity announcements, use a sentence that names the entity, figure and unit, scope, and status together. If a table footnote contains a critical distinction, consider placing a short label next to the value as well. In your audit log, record the claim, its scope, status and date, source URL, and verdict. That makes it easier to revisit a claim when the source changes.
