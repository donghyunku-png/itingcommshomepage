# AI-ready business content: evidence matters more than more FAQs

“Will adding more FAQs make AI recommend our company?”

A question-and-answer format can help customers find information. It does not guarantee an AI recommendation. The first thing to check is not how many questions a page contains, but whether readers can verify its answers.

## 1. Start with clear information, not a secret AI format

Google says established SEO practices remain relevant to AI Overviews and AI Mode. No special optimization or dedicated structured data is required, and structured data should match the visible page. Meeting the requirements does not guarantee crawling, indexing or display. These statements concern Google Search, not a universal rule for every AI service. [1]

Instead of promising that FAQs will automatically make AI learn and recommend a business, aim to give customers accurate, verifiable answers. Model training, inclusion in search results, and citation in an answer should not be described as one interchangeable process.

## 2. Give each answer scope and evidence

Consider this fictional service page. The examples below are illustrative, not actual client results or product commitments.

**A weak answer**

Q. What is GEO?

A. An innovative marketing solution that delivers the best results in the AI era.

**A clearer answer**

Q. What can a GEO assessment examine?

A. It can examine whether a brand is mentioned for defined questions, which sources appear, and whether the business is described accurately. Interpret results alongside the engine, questions, measurement date and number of observations. Distinguish this work from products that promise particular rankings or recommendations.

The second answer explains both the work and its limits. Adding an approved sample report or a methodology page lets readers inspect the approach. This is an editorial technique for clarity, not a formula that guarantees citations.

## 3. Explain the comparison before reporting results

“Performance improved” needs a baseline. For a case approved for public use, document:

- Scope: the brand, product or market studied.
- Period: when the observations were collected.
- Method: what was compared, under which conditions.
- Result: the change supported by the underlying records.
- Limits: seasonality, advertising activity, sample size or other relevant factors.

Do not invent numbers to fill gaps. When client names or results cannot be disclosed, begin with an approved anonymized case or a methodology explanation without performance claims. Check that linked supporting files do not expose confidential information.

Google's people-first content guidance encourages original information, clear sourcing and evidence of the author's background and trustworthiness. E-E-A-T is not itself a specific ranking factor; it should not be presented as a single score a company can manage. [2]

## 4. Start with five pages

Rather than rewriting the whole site, we suggest checking these pages first:

- **About:** Are the company name, address, contacts and business areas consistent?
- **Services:** Are intended customers, deliverables and exclusions clear?
- **Methodology:** Does the page explain how the work is conducted and interpreted?
- **Cases:** Are the evidence and comparison conditions approved for disclosure?
- **FAQs:** Do short, direct answers address questions that actually arise in customer conversations?

Keep key information readable in the page text, not only inside images. Connect related pages. An FAQ should help readers navigate services, methods and evidence rather than stand apart from them.

## 5. Make your explanation verifiable

Writing GEO content is not about repeating sentences that supposedly appeal to AI. A more useful approach is to answer customers' questions and show the supporting evidence and limitations.

Instead of adding ten FAQs today, improve one existing answer with scope and evidence. Start by asking whether the page makes it easier for a reader to make an informed decision.
