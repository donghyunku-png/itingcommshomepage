# Law-firm GEO: design for trust after discovery

People looking for legal services need more than a visible name. They need to understand a firm's actual practice areas, how to request a consultation and the basis and freshness of its information. **Discovery, verification and consultation** should therefore be managed as distinct stages.

ITING Communications proposes a law-firm GEO package connecting GEO Inside observations and analysis with website and content improvements. The following is a scope for discussion, not a client case study or a guarantee of search visibility or legal outcomes. Scope and pricing are available on inquiry.

## 1. Align the official information first

Check the firm's name, offices, contacts, lawyer biographies and actual services against its official website and manageable external profiles. Location references must reflect real offices or services. Qualifications, experience and specialist descriptions need supporting evidence and appropriate review.

Practice-area pages should provide readable explanations of relevant questions, documents to prepare, general procedures and contact routes. Do not turn general explanations into conclusions about individual cases, or use identifiable client information or consultation records without authorization.

## 2. Start with search fundamentals, not special AI code

Google's official guidance says established SEO practices also apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. Check crawl and index access, internal links, useful text and page experience. Structured data should match visible content. A special AI schema or new AI-specific file is not required, and meeting the requirements does not guarantee inclusion or citation.

A useful review therefore goes beyond adding one file. It examines clear service descriptions, accurate FAQs, document structure, mobile consultation journeys, stale information and broken links. Google's guidance should not be represented as the identical policy of every other AI service.

## 3. Separate mentions and citations from consultation outcomes

Agree on practice areas, regions and a question set before measuring. Record the exact question, service and search mode, language, observation time, answer and cited sources. Track name mentions, official-page citations, factual accuracy and recommendation wording separately.

One answer or a selection of favorable questions cannot establish an overall recommendation rate. Repeat comparable observations where possible and retain unsuccessful or unverified results. Inquiries, qualified inquiries and signed engagements are different measures. Connect them only where consent and appropriate privacy practices permit; increased mentions alone do not prove revenue impact.

## 4. Review advertising language and privacy before publication

Article 23 of Korea's Attorney-at-Law Act restricts false, exaggerated or misleading advertising and statements creating unjustified expectations of results. Before publication, a responsible legal reviewer should assess the applicable current law, Korean Bar Association rules and specific wording. GEO analysis and technical checks do not replace that review.

Avoid guaranteed outcomes, unsupported superlatives and unverified comparative or performance claims. Separate public educational explanations from confidential case and consultation records, and consider both permission to use a case and the risk of re-identification.

## 5. Agree on diagnosis, improvements and operation as separate stages

- **Diagnosis:** establish a baseline for official information, practice-area pages, search accessibility and observed AI answers. Deliver evidence, priorities, scope limits and unknowns.
- **Foundation improvements:** agree on website and content changes, review wording and privacy, then implement and verify the approved scope. Define development and content-production responsibilities separately.
- **Observation and operation:** track agreed questions over an agreed period and recommend the next improvements. Paid media and CRM integration are optional scopes requiring separate checks of platform eligibility, permissions, privacy and costs.

Useful starting materials include public company information, actual practice and office details, website-management scope and the designated content reviewer. Confidential case files or client personal data are not needed to begin the discussion.
